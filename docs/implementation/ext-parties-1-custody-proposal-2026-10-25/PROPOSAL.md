## Recommended backend and resources

Recommend a **Platform-owned timed-erasure backend using dedicated on-premises Thales Luna PCIe HSM 7 device pairs with a reviewed custom Functionality Module (FM)**, coupled to a separately operated lifecycle authority. Allocate a distinct pair per environment, plus a replacement pair per environment for maintenance. This is a new backend engineering/procurement proposal, not an assertion that stock Luna firmware provides timed destruction. No commercial SKU, firmware capability, price, license or delivery slot has been accepted.

Thales documents [custom FM code inside the HSM boundary](https://thalesdocs.com/gphsm/luna/7/docs/network/Content/Product_Overview/fms.htm), and the [FM development/acceptance process](https://www.thalesdocs.com/gphsm/luna/7/docs/network/Content/FM_SDK/FM_Development_Cycle.htm). Those documents establish programmability, **not autonomous per-unit zeroization**. The initial funded milestone is a vendor-supported feasibility demonstration of that exact requirement, including power loss, clock failure, firmware rollback, memory retention and all replicas. If unsupported, reject this backend before provider implementation or production purchase; this proposal supplies no qualified substitute and cannot relax the accepted policy.

The volatile-root choice intentionally limits recoverability: genuine ciphertext/key-envelope/lifecycle backups restore only while both original root devices survive and the unit remains live. The required positive unexpired restore control must pass through those devices. A total root-device loss is permanent data loss, not a reason to reconstruct a root. If required recovery objectives demand recovery after that loss, Q1 must reject this design or commission a separately proven deadline-safe root recovery mechanism. No generally restorable root backup may be introduced as an operational shortcut.

Maintenance must drain an immutable pair without migrating its roots: stop new admissions to that pair, admit new units to the environment's separately qualified replacement pair, keep the original devices and firmware powered until their last original deadline (up to 365 days after last admission), then verify all original receipts before servicing them. Firmware updates, reset, host reboots and replacement during that interval are destructive to live roots and require a separate incident/data-loss decision. A security patch that cannot wait for drain may make this backend operationally unacceptable; Q1/Q4 must decide that before purchase. Device-wide zeroization cannot be substituted for selective expiry while unrelated units remain live.

Capacity is a feasibility gate, not inferred from a pair count. Initially limit an isolated engineering cohort to **100 live units and two generations per unit**, with no production admission. Vendor measurements must cover protected volatile-memory overhead per unit/generation, 365-day unit arrival forecasts, copy/receipt overhead, secure-clock timer limits, worst-case simultaneous expiry, admission/read throughput and AEAD payload sizes. Production owners must accept measured limits and headroom per environment before enabling any admission. Capacity exhaustion refuses new reservations before allocating keys; it never evicts still-live roots or spills them to disk. Add device pairs only through a newly approved complete resource target.

## All-copy expiry and nonrollback acceptance requirements

Admission cannot set `SourceExpiryEnforced`, `RestoreSafe` or `DerivedCopiesCovered` until independent acceptance proves all of the following. These are implementation obligations, not installed guarantees:

1. Persist one idempotent original reservation before root creation; reconcile crash/lost acknowledgment without allocating replacement evidence. Authenticated unit context includes full aggregate/environment/instance, policy/purpose, original instant/deadline, evidence/admission revision and key generation.
2. The FM enforces immutable original deadlines and irreversible per-unit/generation zeroization independent of workload/cleanup/backend connectivity. Before/at/after deadline tests must demonstrate no release at expiry and no recoverable material afterward. All powered/unpowered and clock-uncertain states require proven treatment. A delayed boot-time purge or temporary read denial does not satisfy exact expiry. Failure to prove this prevents any admission.
3. Every C01–C16 class has a real location/generation/owner or authenticated absence: source; current/embedded snapshots; older versions; projections; caches; broker/outbox/DLQ/replay; spools/exports; telemetry; replicas; native backups; off-site/immutable copies; raw/wrapped/escrowed/rotated keys; key backups/seal dependencies; drill/quarantine copies; work/receipts; F1 continuations. Register/fence copies before release. Retain only ciphertext in ordinary backups. Root loss makes all associated wrapped copies unusable; plaintext copies or independently decryptable escrow invalidate the design.
4. Copy admission and destruction share the current authority fence. Freeze inventory generation, settle/cancel in-flight writes and independently verify each class outcome before one original final receipt. Unknown/missing acknowledgments stay Pending. Cleanup uses existing five-second waits; that wait is not the cryptographic expiry SLA.
5. Old workload incarnations and restored data/key/lifecycle cuts cannot lower current authority. Quarantine remains closed until authority lineage, original deadlines, epoch and owner hooks reconcile. No administrator override, device cloning, FM downgrade or old issuer can recreate decrypting authority. Signed checkpoints need a surviving monotonic witness; a signature on an old record is insufficient.
6. Proposed operational bounds for owner review: authority age at release at most 1 second; accepted authenticated clock uncertainty at most 100 ms, with release only when the entire uncertainty interval precedes expiry; backend calls at most 5 seconds; cleanup retries every 30 seconds; alert after 2 minutes; final receipt within 15 minutes of restored connectivity. These are proposed bounds, not permissions for material to survive expiry. Physical timed-erasure precision and availability within the final uncertainty interval require explicit Q1/Q2/Q4 acceptance; no source clock simulation proves them.

Root destruction alone cannot erase plaintext already copied into a paused application's memory. Q1/Q3/Q6 feasibility therefore also requires a closed plaintext-holder boundary: hardware-contained AEAD, independently inventoried transient output holders, nonpageable/non-dumpable buffers with enforced deadline retirement, cancellation of in-flight decryption/release, and no ordinary application cache, log, immutable managed-string copy or export retaining actor evidence past its original deadline. Pause/suspend the owning processes before expiry, cross expiry with workloads/network unavailable, resume them and independently inspect their retained memory/copy outcomes. Prove no late release or recoverable plaintext/DEK through device or host crash dumps, snapshots or debugger paths. **The current .NET host paths do not establish those memory guarantees; no attested transient-holder implementation is delivered.** If the deployed plaintext path cannot meet the accepted all-copy scope, this design fails feasibility even if the HSM can zeroize roots. An owner-approved scope interpretation cannot be invented by the adapter.
