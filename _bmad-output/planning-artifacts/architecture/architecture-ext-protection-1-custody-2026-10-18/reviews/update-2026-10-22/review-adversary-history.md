# Adversary review — retained history and recovery

Date: 2026-10-22. Scope: current custody spine and human design, reconciled against the production actor-history lifecycle contract, its decision memlog, October 7 cleanup contract, and existing unit/receipt and SDK source contracts. No deliverable edits or execution qualification.

**Verdict: preserve the design; sharpen two remaining owner choices before treating the decision packet as fully reviewable.** No accepted-policy regression, falsely accepted AWS backend, or unsafe production path was found. Production admission refusal and the explicit open Q decisions prevent the counterexamples below from being qualified implementations. They are contract choices that independent teams could prepare incompatibly while waiting for approval, not permission to bypass the stated gates.

## H1 — Name the plaintext-consumer contract decision, not only the expiry-key mechanism

**Severity:** medium; owner-decision completeness. **Owners:** Platform history custody, EventStore SDK/source reader, Parties codec/domain, and privacy/security; extend Q1/Q4/Q6.

**Locations:** spine AD-27, line 122; design lines 87–89, Q6 line 130 and history-boundary proof line 144. Existing `IIdentityHistoryCustody.UnprotectEventAsync` transfers detached `byte[]` ownership to the caller; `UnprotectSnapshotAsync` returns arbitrary `object`. `RetainedIdentityHistorySourceReader` lines 152–173 deserializes an event into an ordinary managed domain object and serializes another readable byte copy; line 175 clears only the provider result buffer.

The architecture correctly requires every plaintext holder to be covered by the deadline. Its proposed non-exportable key boundary does not itself control plaintext returned by the seven-method SDK. Current source ownership wording requires clearing readable bytes after use, with no deadline-bearing handle, consumer enrollment, or all-copy completion agreement. Deserialization can create managed actor/provenance strings beyond the provider's buffers. AD-27's prohibition on reusable key escape therefore leaves a separate implementation gap even if H1's key primitive becomes available.

**Two independently prepared units:** Platform proposes remote non-exportable encryption plus a current check before returning payload bytes. EventStore proposes existing detached-byte ownership, typed deserialization and clear-after-use; Parties proposes the existing object snapshot contract. Each can account for its immediate buffers while no one owns the typed/forwarded plaintext deadline. Their interfaces do not compose into the all-holder guarantee. Literal AD-27 must reject this combined candidate; the owner table should identify the missing choice explicitly so no one mistakes this for ordinary DI/bootstrap work.

**Requested amendment:** make Q6 explicitly choose a plaintext-processing/release contract with a named owner for each recipient, managed/serialized copy and deadline-crossing in-flight operation. Give reviewable alternatives: (a) qualified consumer execution and deadline-bound buffer/object ownership throughout both hosts, possibly requiring a versioned contract; (b) move the relevant processing inside a controlled boundary with a versioned result/query contract; (c) refuse affected admission/read paths until either qualifies. Neither (a) nor (b) is a proven implementation. Require an outage/stall spanning expiry after plaintext release, not only during the provider await. Do not silently weaken the policy to future-read denial or a key-only guarantee.

## H2 — Give Q3/Q7 concrete receipt alternatives and distinguish post-expiry lookup from immutable-original preservation

**Severity:** medium; review-readiness and contract reconciliation. **Owners:** Parties privacy/domain, Platform custody/recovery, EventStore SDK and cleanup/work owners; Q3/Q7.

**Locations:** spine AD-28, line 128; design line 117 and Q3/Q7 lines 128/131. The production lifecycle contract's “Durable authority, copies and receipts” section requires an original receipt surviving retries/restart/lost acknowledgement and an owner mechanism to retrieve it. Existing `IdentityHistoryDestructionReceipt` lines 18–27 requires the full `IdentityHistoryCustodyUnit`; its `Unit` at line 88 contains Party aggregate identity, original evidence ID, effective instant and expiry. The current architecture correctly identifies these as potentially linkable and expressly forbids rewriting/resigning the immutable original at expiry.

The packet names the format and retention problem but gives no actual format/lookup alternatives for reviewers to accept. This matters particularly when the final observation or lost acknowledgement is recovered after expiry: the original DTO requires linkable fields whose retaining key/mapping must already have expired if privacy classifies them as history. Keeping an old exact receipt and making a new minimized receipt are different contracts; a numeric retention value alone cannot reconcile them.

**Two independently prepared units:** a custody unit designs same-deadline protection of all `Unit` fields plus a separate minimal operational attestation; an owner recovery unit designs existing full-original receipt lookup and equality after restart. Both can preserve immutability and claim future minimization work, yet recovery cannot consume the first design with the existing DTO, and extending its decryption key to support the second design breaches the stated history rule. Q3/Q7 currently block this combination correctly; show the contract fork to the owners now.

**Requested amendment:** enumerate at least two reviewable paths, both explicitly unaccepted: (a) a versioned terminal-verification format and post-expiry lookup identity that owners demonstrate carries no retained attribution, while the unchanged full original remains deadline-protected; (b) a versioned lifecycle/owner-recovery contract that ends per-unit linkable-original lookup at expiry and retains only approved non-attribution proof. State what retry/lost-ack outcome callers receive after expiry under each path, which mappings/journals/public verifiers survive, and the numerical retention period for each. If either cannot meet accepted evidence obligations, keep admission blocked. Retention of the current linkable original beyond expiry would require explicit policy change and is not an eligible silent fallback. Interaction batch originals remain governed by AD-18/22/23 and must not be truncated by this history choice.

## Review limit

This review checks the consistency contract and source seams. It does not establish a feasible exact-expiry primitive, independent provider-internal deletion proof, installed target, authority identity, owner principal, or production availability. No true pair of fully qualifying implementations was found that both obey the literal all-holder/deadline and minimization rules while reviving expired history; the two findings concern making the unresolved contract alternatives concrete enough for owner approval.

## Narrow fix verification — 2026-10-22

**H1 resolved at architecture/decision-packet scope.** AD-27 now explicitly covers detached caller bytes, deserialized objects/strings, forwarded results and snapshots, and states that current returns supply no deadline-completion proof. The human design's P1/P2 alternatives name a versioned enforced ownership/processing boundary versus qualification of the complete existing consumer graph; callbacks/disposal conventions alone do not count as enforcement. Q6 owns the selection and actual plaintext-lifetime proof. Admission remains refused until the selected path qualifies. This addresses the missing owner choice without claiming either construction is feasible.

**H2 resolved at architecture/decision-packet scope.** AD-28 and the human design's R1/R2 alternatives distinguish exact retained sealed bytes plus minimal independently verifiable terminal proof from an expiry-bounded full-readable lookup contract. Both require a versioned accepted contract, classify locators/indexes/proof fields rather than assuming opaque IDs are safe, preserve the original operation/proof after response loss, and prohibit recreating an identity key or resigning the original. Q3/Q7 retain numeric schedules, privacy classification and format acceptance as explicit owner inputs. The existing full `Unit` DTO is expressly insufficient for equivalent post-expiry plaintext lookup. Independent interaction-original obligations remain intact.

**Remaining unresolved reviewer findings: none.** Owner selections, actual lifetime enforcement, compatible receipt implementation, privacy classification and qualification remain deliberately open delivery gates; resolving these review findings does not select a backend or establish production availability. This was a narrow verification of the two requested fixes, not a new broad review.
