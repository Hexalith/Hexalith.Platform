## High — the native contract remains a hard gate (discuss/owner decision)

The historical provider decision requires a native transaction to validate roots/pins/revisions, destroy all targets or none, and retain the ordered irreversible vector. `ICustodyKeyLifecycleProvider` requires current control in the physical operation; `IAtomicDeletionManifestProvider` requires exact complete manifest/pin checks, all-or-none effect and durable originals. B1/B3/B5 conditional metadata transactions plus B4 KMS deletion do not provide that single native boundary. The draft's decisive-limit paragraph and OA-1 now state this accurately and do not silently adopt equivalence.

Required disposition: retain OA-1 as an explicit change of the accepted criterion, with the contract-owner acceptance recorded separately before binding production adapters. If that change is not accepted, the conclusion is “no qualifying backend established; this candidate rejected under the unchanged criterion.” Architecture approval of components alone cannot satisfy this gate. Do not count F0/F2 shared-cut behavior as proof of literal native atomicity.

## High — current authority and stale native permissions are not yet one enforceable protocol (discuss; add experiment)

AD-18/AD-24 and the restart/provisioning passages require old-instance grants to be fenced, unknown native requests reconciled, direct decrypt/rewrap bypass denied, and all copies registered before effect. The draft does not select the native credential/grant/egress ownership and drain protocol that makes those rules hold. A DynamoDB head CAS fences head publication; it cannot itself revoke a worker's outstanding KMS `Decrypt`/`ReEncrypt` request. An old or late `ReEncrypt` can place a selected X in the adopted survivor epoch, invalidating the claim that deleting the old E is its only surviving path. These are different failure cases from a stale worker failing CAS.

[KMS documents](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/kms/latest/developerguide/grants.html) that grant creation, retirement and revocation propagate eventually, and a revoked grantee can retain use temporarily. [ReEncrypt](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/kms/latest/APIReference/API_ReEncrypt.html) uses separate source/destination permissions and has no DynamoDB revision parameter. A fixed credential-revocation sleep is therefore not a proven quiescence barrier.

Required disposition: name the native permission owner and exact per-incarnation grant/session/endpoint restrictions, close every generation's permissions before arm, define how in-flight source/decrypt/reencrypt attempts are durably registered and resolved, and define admissible evidence of native/runtime quiescence. Specify where the current enrollment/TableId/incarnation record lives and who may advance it when the primary table is unavailable; “outside the restored set” is a placement constraint, not its current-authority mechanism. If trusted runtime/holder termination rather than native fencing is essential, state it expressly as an OA-4 trusted assumption and a production stop condition. Add an F3 case that delays a pre-freeze native `ReEncrypt` response across B3/worker replacement and proves no selected ciphertext reaches any surviving epoch; test actual revoked credentials, not only application head denial.

## Medium — load-bearing private seam and actor bounds are not yet a build contract (autofix by exact seed, or explicitly gate implementation)

OA-5 asks maintainers to adopt an “exact” private outer format, guard handoff and lease completion protocol, but the decision defers their closed fields/canonical byte encoding and proof admission semantics to later agreement/F1. OA-3 likewise asks for a bounded projection/archive plan while the inspected actor stores at most 1,000 registered keys and 10,000 requests and forbids dropping originals. Two adapters built now could choose incompatible handoff identities, generation/checkpoint layouts, completion acknowledgements or archival retrieval semantics. These are real cross-unit divergence points, not routine implementation freedom.

Required disposition: either supply a companion contract seed now (closed request/proof/checkpoint/envelope and signed original identities; exact predecessor/guard/B0 binding; native generation registration; completion/termination evidence; actor projection/archive ownership and original lookup) or clearly limit “implementation-ready” to bounded feasibility work and make those outputs a gate before parallel adapter implementation. Preserve `candidate-custody-key-wrap-v1`, original K(t,v)/KekVersion and opaque registration semantics; the outer X gate must remain provider private. Do not solve scale by silently lifting the actor limits or pruning exact originals.

## Good-spine rubric

The named paradigm, owner boundaries, parent IDs, stable AD IDs, minimal structural diagram, explicit new deployment/recovery classes, transaction separation, operational failure domains, lifecycle treatment, original outcomes and approval inventory are effective. No whole required operational dimension is silently missing. The X-over-C0 hierarchy preserves the accepted tenant KEK contract and has a sound conditional erasure argument if every decrypting path and holder really closes.

The unresolved enforceability issues are the native-fencing protocol and the exact cross-unit private seam/actor storage policy above. Deferring those is safe only as a gate to feasibility, not as permission to implement incompatible adapters. Technology pins and provider-media/runtime cleanup claims are correctly separated into verified observations versus unproved assumptions; this review does not independently qualify cloud version compatibility or physical vendor copies.

Recommended gate outcome: retain the decision as an owner-review recommendation, apply the targeted seam/fencing clarifications, and keep OA-1–OA-6 plus F0–F3 as explicit production blockers. No acceptance status changes follow.

## Focused follow-up after revision — 2026-10-20

Re-read the revised decision's native-key broker, private protocol/first-release bounds, B1–B3 tenant freeze and successor adoption, copy inventory and F3, plus revised AD-17/AD-20/AD-23/AD-24.

The native-call finding is addressed at proposed-design level. A sole broker has KMS use permissions; one durable send commitment never retries an unknown send; SDK and HTTP retries/redirects are disabled; actual incarnation/I/O/buffer closure or independently accepted termination precedes arm. Unknown staging destinations are tainted, never adopted and permanently deleted before arm; unknown current/adopted/unbounded destinations block qualification. The adopted epoch switch and complete tenant admission freeze prevent concurrent producers from reopening the old epoch. These rules avoid claiming that a head CAS, credential revocation or fixed wait fences a late native request. Actual permission isolation, host termination, SDK/TLS cleanup and full native closure remain appropriately OA-4/F2/F3 acceptance proof, not verified guarantees.

The seam/storage finding is addressed for an owner proposal. Exact outer AAD order, inner canonical format, source-authenticated proof fields and checkpoint/head binding now constrain independent implementation; golden vectors and maximum-byte tests remain a pre-build compatibility gate. Keeping actual actor limits, forbidding automatic archive/shard/eviction and reserving finite completion capacity is a concrete conservative first-release choice. F1 failure stops implementation rather than silently expanding limits. Export-key paths are now explicitly separate from interaction DEKs and unqualified affected export paths block all-copy completion.

**Remaining medium clarification:** authority-table loss/replacement still refers to current TableId/incarnation enrollment “outside the restored set” without naming its current-record store and advancement/fencing protocol. Normal app restore against a surviving pinned authority table is coherent. A restored/new table should either be explicitly unsupported in the first release (table/authority loss stays fail-closed, no new incarnation enrollment or genesis) or require a separately accepted exact independent enrollment protocol before implementation of that recovery branch. Owner/operator discretion alone must not turn a restored checkpoint into proof of latest authority. This clarification does not require infrastructure or register changes.

**Follow-up verdict:** pass as an unapproved owner-decision proposal, with the medium table-replacement clarification retained as a gate. The unchanged literal native criterion remains unmet under OA-1; no production-binding, availability, complete backend or observed all-copy guarantee is established.
