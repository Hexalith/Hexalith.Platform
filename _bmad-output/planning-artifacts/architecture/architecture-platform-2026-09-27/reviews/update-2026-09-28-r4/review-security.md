## Cross-rule interactions

- **A restore may leave signed grants absent without authorizing recovery to add them.** The two-way monitor then keeps the production stop set. Administrator re-application is the explicit route to convergence; a deputy-led recovery can still reopen the previously allowed ingress state under the accepted recovery policy. Reopening and promotion clearance have distinct authorities and criteria.
- **Accepting a stop cause cannot waive the admission-match requirement.** Both ordinary clearance and approved empty/degraded attempts require the Administrator reviews, and the G1 qualification row expressly tests refusal while admission differs. The per-cause mechanism therefore does not create an admission bypass.
- **A failed repair preserves removal and revocation protections.** Named data restore keeps the live realm and tenant-key store in place, invalidates any restored admission projection until rebuild, and executes the same admission-denial verification before reopening. Compatibility evidence alone grants no automatic rollback on the degraded path.
- **A reserved epoch does not authorize workload mutations.** Reserving it allows the control actions needed to fence or drain the previous owner. Operational acceptance follows that barrier. This resolves the earlier circularity without allowing two mutation owners.
- **The deputy's removal-only reconciliation does not create independent admission policy.** It applies Administrator-signed records and the documented recovery rule. New grants, new revocation decisions, release approvals and promotion-stop clears remain outside the deputy's authority.

The review does not treat those assigned but unimplemented controls as demonstrated security. No additional gate is required by this review.
