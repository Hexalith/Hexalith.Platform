## Findings resolved during the gate

The following records preserve the adversarial examples and corrective contract. Both are closed by the latest parent-authored changes; they are not outstanding requirements.

### RA-01 — Name the current management authority used after restoring an older manager

Status: closed. Cluster management now names Administrator-approved management grant/revocation records retained independently outside the primary failure domain, complete auditable lineage through the failure cut, fail-closed treatment of missing/conflicting/gapped current authority, and Administrator-only grant changes. 4.26 and its Epic criterion, CAP-9, the migration spec and qualification/deployment runbooks adopt that same source. `QUALIFICATION.md` specifically tests a post-cut management revocation whose source instance is lost. Recovery only removes stale access or reissues credentials within an existing scoped entitlement, preserving deputy powers.

- **location:** `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md`, Cluster management (lines 247 and 251), AD-6/AD-12 and Recovery sequence step 5; `epics.md`, Story 8.11's Rancher criterion; `eng/cluster-management/RANCHER.md`, Backup and isolated restore step 3.
- **trigger_condition:** A Rancher operator role or management credential is revoked after the management backup cut, then the manager and its live approval/revocation state are lost. The documents require later revocations to be reapplied and current approved grants to bound restored access, but do not designate an independently retained current authority for those management decisions.
- **independent implementation pair:** The account/role implementation treats the current live manager and its external identity memberships as management authority; the recovery implementation treats the approved operator map and retained configuration export as its authority. Both keep AD-6 production admission separate, enforce MFA/private access and deny known revoked credentials, but no contract requires their management-grant and revocation frontier to coincide. A direct manager revocation can be absent from the latter's retained inputs. Using the recovery snapshot as current can restore unwanted authority; refusing every uncertain role can leave the deputy unable to complete the qualified manager recovery. This is an authority-source gap, not a request to standardize an entire Rancher resource schema.
- **guard_snippet:** Amend the existing AD-6/AD-12 convention: “Administrator owns an independently durable current management-grant/revocation record, available to the deputy without either manager. Qualification names its identity binding, retention and update/freshness contract. Restored manager users, bindings, tokens and registration authority are reconciled to that record; restored state never supplies current authority. Missing or incomplete authority prevents manager access/reconnection; recovery cannot add grants or expand deputy powers.” Reuse the existing signed-record/custody machinery where appropriate. Bind 4.26's operator map and 4.28/8.11 recovery assertions to the same source.
- **potential_consequence:** Post-cut management revocations cannot be proved preserved after manager loss, or account and recovery implementations disagree on who may administer the restored manager. The initial isolated revoked-credential test can pass for one known revocation without proving general current-authority reconstruction.

### RA-02 — Separate manager readiness from the native workload restore gate

Status: closed. Recovery sequence step 2 now reproduces workload-required shared infrastructure and expressly lets Rancher restoration proceed independently of native workload recovery or verification. Management remains fenced until current authority and target identity reconcile; necessary management steps remain included in the applicable RTO accounting. The migration spec and deployment/recovery runbook carry the same ordering. Initial 4.28 qualification before staging/G1 remains required.

- **location:** `ARCHITECTURE-SPINE.md`, Cluster management (lines 247–251), Release tiers' Shared infrastructure row (line 259), AD-12 and Recovery sequence step 2 (line 339); `epics.md`, Stories 4.28 and 8.17; `eng/cluster-management/RANCHER.md`, Backup and isolated restore step 4.
- **trigger_condition:** The workload cluster needs replacement-capacity recovery while the management VM cannot be restored or its manager cannot become Ready. The native path is independently usable, but Recovery sequence step 2 reproduces “shared infrastructure,” whose tier now contains the manager, before step 4 restores workloads.
- **independent implementation pair:** The shared-infrastructure restorer treats completion of step 2 as requiring every listed shared service, including Rancher, to be Ready before the data phase. The native recovery implementation prepares the retained management definitions in quarantine but completes workload restoration and service verification through the native API while Rancher remains unavailable. Both use the same AD-12 recovery inputs, direct access, qualified release and stale-authority fencing; they differ on whether management readiness is a prerequisite. Independent access alone does not settle this dependency in the recovery graph.
- **guard_snippet:** Tighten AD-12's management convention and step 2: “Workload recovery restores and verifies its required shared dependencies through the independent native path. Rancher/management-cluster readiness and re-registration are not prerequisites for native workload restoration or the recorded workload reopening. Management remains isolated/fenced until its own restore, current-authority and identity checks pass. Record its outcome and every required step/substitution under the applicable management recovery plan and integrated Epic 8 timing evidence.” Preserve 4.28 qualification before staging/G1 and all existing workload integrity, authority and RTO requirements.
- **potential_consequence:** An otherwise recoverable workload outage waits for manager recovery, making the new console an operational recovery dependency despite the approved 4.28 intent; independently built restore units can deadlock or disagree on the acceptable working-service outcome.
